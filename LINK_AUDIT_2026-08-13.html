What I checked

Coverage

All 49 pages in sitemap.xml, downloaded and parsed — 5,373 anchors, 91 unique destinations. Every destination was status-checked following redirects to the final response, which matters: the old-site links return 308 on the first hop and look healthy until you follow them to the 404.

Relative hrefs were resolved against the page they appear on, not the site root. Framer uses ./ and ../ prefixes heavily, and the same href means different things on / and on /tech/reporting. Resolved that way, all internal navigation and every fragment target checks out — the problems below are content links, not the nav.

Bonus — this answers last week’s open question

These ten URLs reveal the old site’s structure

When I audited indexing I could not determine the previous URL taxonomy, because web.archive.org is unreachable from here. I guessed /blog/{slug}. That guess was wrong, and these hardcoded links are the real evidence:

/marketing/{slug}/  ·  /business-administration/{slug}/  ·  /sales-demo/

The old site organised posts by topic category, not under /blog/. So the redirect map you need is /marketing/* and /business-administration/* → /blogs/*, plus /sales-demo/ → /book-a-demo. Still confirm the full inventory in Search Console — there are almost certainly more categories than these two — but the pattern is now known rather than assumed.

3. The rest

4 findings
P1-1 P1 Dead CTA

A button with text and no href at all

/blogs/decoding-local-seo-putting-your-business-on-the-map-in-2024 
button text : "Explore SMRT Marketing"
href        : absent entirely
instances   : 2 (desktop + mobile variant)

An <a> with no href is inert: it does nothing on click, it is not keyboard-focusable, and a screen reader does not announce it as a link. This is a live CTA on a blog post that silently goes nowhere.

Fix

Point it at /tech/marketing, which is presumably what it was meant to open.

P2-1 P2 Malformed URL All 49 pages

The footer’s Couture link has two literal spaces in its fragment

Footer, Solutions column, on every page 
link text : "— Couture Dry Cleaning"
href      : ./special-services#Couture Dry Cleaning     (6 pages)
            ../special-services#Couture Dry Cleaning    (43 pages)

siblings  : #hotels  #uniforms  #restoration     <- correctly formed

The target id="Couture Dry Cleaning" does exist on /special-services, spaces and all, so most browsers will scroll to it and the link appears to work. But the URL is invalid as written: spaces must be percent-encoded. It breaks in link crawlers, email clients, analytics reports and anything that normalises URLs — curl cannot even issue the request. The three sibling links use clean lowercase fragments, so this one is simply inconsistent.

Fix

Rename the section id on /special-services to couture and update the href to #couture. One id change, one link change, fixed on all 49 pages at once since it is a shared footer.

P2-2 P2 Semantics All 49 pages

147 navigation dropdown triggers are <a> elements with no href

Products, Solutions and Resources in the main nav are anchors with no href — three per page across all 49 pages. They work with a mouse because JavaScript handles the click, but an <a> without href is not in the tab order and is not announced as an interactive control.

This is the same concern the pre-launch audit raised about the dropdowns, now confirmed in the markup on every page.

Fix

These are menu triggers, not links — they should be <button> elements with aria-expanded. In Framer that usually means using the built-in menu component rather than a custom text layer with a click handler.

What is not broken

Checked and clear

Worth stating explicitly, so nobody re-investigates these.

  • All internal navigation resolves correctly. Framer’s ./ and ../ hrefs look alarming — ./point-of-sale on a /tech/* page, ./tech/point-of-sale on a root page — but resolved against their own page, all 75 on-site destinations return 200.
  • Every fragment target exists. All ten anchors (#pos-features, #route-features, #payment-features, #assembly-features, #vision, #hotels, #uniforms, #restoration, #special-services, and yes #Couture Dry Cleaning) resolve to real ids.
  • All 49 sitemap URLs return 200.
  • No http:// links. Everything external is HTTPS.
  • No empty href="", no href="#", no javascript:void(0).
  • All 8 tel: and mailto: links are well-formed — six phone numbers in E.164, plus support@ and marketing@.

Three that look broken in my crawl but are not

My requests were rate-limited or bot-blocked by these hosts, which is normal behaviour and not evidence of a bad link. I am not claiming these are broken — they need a check from a real browser:

  • instagram.com/smrtsystems/ — returned 429, then a login redirect
  • youtu.be/sKMifdZBOCs — hit a Google “sorry” interstitial; the sibling video link resolved fine
  • facebook.com/smrtsystems — 302 to a login page, which Facebook does for unauthenticated crawlers

The support.smrtsystems.com help-centre links were also unreachable from this environment by egress policy, apart from the two 301s noted above which I could observe. Spot-check the support links from a browser.

Fix order

By cost of leaving it
  1. Repoint the 33 framer.link/P8mvaz1 buttons to /book-a-demo. Six pages, ~17 distinct buttons. These are demo CTAs sending prospects to a Framer sign-up page.
  2. Fix the two “Book a SMRT demo” buttons pointing at /sales-demo/ (404). Also demo CTAs, also dead.
  3. Repoint the 15 featured-card image links on /blogs from old-site URLs to their /blogs/{slug} equivalents.
  4. Give “Explore SMRT Marketing” an href — probably /tech/marketing.
  5. Rename the Couture section id to couture and fix the footer href. Clears 49 malformed URLs in one change.
  6. Update the three renamed external links.
  7. Convert the three nav dropdown triggers to <button> with aria-expanded.
  8. Search the project for framer.link, duplicate=, via= and www.smrtsystems.com as a final sweep. The last of those is the one to keep an eye on — any absolute link to your own old domain is a 404 waiting to be found.

Method and limits

Confidence

Measured

49 pages fetched from sitemap.xml; 5,373 anchors parsed with their link text, aria-label, and nearest preceding heading; 91 unique destinations resolved per-page and status-checked with redirects followed to the final response. Fragment targets verified against the actual id attributes on each target page.

Judgement calls

The suggested replacement for each dead blog URL is my slug match, not a confirmed editorial mapping — two have no exact equivalent and need a human decision. That the featured-card links come from a CMS field is inferred from their position and their lack of link text. “Explore SMRT Marketing” pointing at /tech/marketing is a guess from the button text.

Not covered

Only pages in sitemap.xml. If unlisted pages exist they were not crawled — though the internal link graph turned up no on-site destinations outside the 49, so the sitemap looks complete. Links injected by JavaScript after load are invisible to a static fetch; given unicornStudio proved capable of hiding from the served HTML, a browser-based pass would be a reasonable double-check. Social and help-centre links could not be verified from this environment.
